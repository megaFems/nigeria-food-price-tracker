# Working with the data: difficulties and inconsistencies

_Generated by `pipeline/report_quality.py`. Source: WFP via HDX (CC BY-IGO)._

What made the data hard to work with while building this tracker, and how each point was handled. The files do not say why they happened, so causes are not assumed.

## Gaps
- 20 of the 25 basket foods have no North-East price at all from 2022-06 to 2023-01 (8 months). _Handled: left blank and shaded on charts._
- 11 of 14 states had stopped by 2023-01. Adamawa stops in 2025-11. _Handled: no national claim is made._
- From July 2017 the number of markets reporting in a month ranges from 5 to 38. _Handled: the index compares like with like._
- 5,520 of 59,317 North-East prices (9.3%) come from a food and month with fewer than 10 markets. _Handled: flagged._

## Prices that look wrong
- 216 prices (0.4%) are above 5x or below 1/5 of the typical price that month. _Handled: flagged, left out of the index._
- Milk powder, Borno, typical price per 400 g: 2022-03: 1,350, 2022-04: 15,290, 2022-05: 33,750, 2023-02: 1,880 naira. No reading exists between May 2022 and February 2023. _Handled: not counted._
- In all, 2,460 of 52,314 basket prices since July 2017 are suspected, most of them spinach. _Handled: shown with their effect in the findings report, not counted in any final figure._
- 32 dollar prices imply an exchange rate more than 5x away from the typical rate that month. _Handled: the dollar column is never used._

## Yobe, May 2021: prices that look assigned to the wrong foods

## Spinach
The typical price sits near 50 naira for most of 2017 to 2023, while Borno is 5 to 9x higher from April to November 2019. _Handled: not used in the index._

## Checking against a second source
Two National Bureau of Statistics tables (Selected Food Price Watch, November 2024 and January 2025) were checked. Neither covers sugar, bananas, powdered milk, oranges, salt or spinach. Both give zone averages and the highest and lowest state rather than every state, and the food list and some pack sizes change between them.

## For anyone using this data

- Compare each price with the months before and after, not only with other markets: a whole month can be off.
- Check that a price is plausible for its stated pack size.
- Compare the same markets over time, because the reporting markets change.
- Leave gaps blank, and say so on the chart.

## Context

Much of this data comes from areas affected by conflict, where collecting prices is hard, and this tracker could not exist without it. The points above have not yet been raised with the publisher.
